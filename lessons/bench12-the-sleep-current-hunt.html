Bench lab 12: The sleep current hunt

Measuring and reducing a microcontroller's sleep current

By the end of this lab you can measure a microcontroller's own supply current on a Nucleo board, compare Run, Sleep and Stop 2, and find a floating input that leaks current.

Where to put the meter

Lesson 75 calculated battery life from a datasheet's sleep current, but a real board rarely reaches that figure on the first try, because one pin or one mode choice is enough to waste most of the battery. This lab puts a meter in the supply and hunts the waste down. It is optional, needing the NUCLEO-U545RE-Q from bench lab 9 and a multimeter with a µA range, it does not count towards your progress, lesson 75 staying complete without it, and it takes about 50 minutes.

The Nucleo powers the STM32 through a jumper labelled IDD, JP4, and UM3062 section 6.4.6 says to remove the jumper and connect an ammeter in its place, so that the meter carries the microcontroller's current and nothing else on the board (ST UM3062). The ST-LINK, the power LED and the regulators are on the other side of it.

  1. Unplug the USB cable, then pull JP4 off and keep it safe.
  2. Set the meter to its mA range first and connect its leads to the two JP4 pins, red to one and black to the other, swapping them if the reading is negative.
  3. Plug the USB cable back in, and the chip starts through the meter.

Start on the mA range, because a meter's µA range puts a larger resistor in series (its burden voltage), and with a few mA flowing through it the chip's supply can sag enough to reset it. Switch to µA only for the sleep readings, and back before you wake the chip. Some meters break the circuit for a moment when you switch ranges, so if the board resets, put the meter on µA before plugging in and read Stop 2 only.

Run, Sleep and Stop 2

Lesson 75 described the STM32F1's modes, and the U5 has the same idea with more steps (Sleep, Stop and Standby), of which this lab uses three.

  • Run: the core spins in a loop at 4 MHz.
  • Sleep: __WFI() with SLEEPDEEP clear (WFI). The core's clock stops; the bus and the peripheral clocks keep running.
  • Stop 2: __WFI() with SLEEPDEEP set and PWR_CR1.LPMS = 010. Almost every clock stops, SRAM and registers keep their contents, and an unmasked EXTI line (B1 on PC13 here) wakes the chip. It resumes at the instruction after WFI, running from MSIS again.

Expect Run in the hundreds of µA to a few mA at this clock, and Stop 2 in single-digit µA when nothing leaks. Your numbers are the result, and the datasheet's typical figures are the target.

On the board: hunt

  1. Build projects/bench12-sleep-current/starter, flash it and open the VCP. Connect a jumper wire to PB0 (ST morpho CN7 pin 34, Arduino A3) and leave its other end loose, because the program reads PB0 at start-up as if it were a configuration jumper.
  2. The VCP says mode RUN, so read the meter. Press B1 for mode LOW POWER, read again, and touch the loose wire with your finger while you watch the meter.
  3. The starter's low-power mode is a plain WFI. Write down both readings as the before of your report.

Now remove the waste one change at a time, measuring after each one.

  1. The mode. Set LPMS to Stop 2 and set SCB->SCR's SLEEPDEEP bit before WFI, clearing it after waking. The PWR registers need their clock, RCC_AHB3ENR_PWREN.
  2. The floating input. PB0 stays a digital input after it has been read, and a loose wire picks up mains hum and your hand's charge, holding the input buffer's voltage between the rails, where both of its transistors conduct a little, bench lab 2's floating input appearing now as a current. After reading the pin, put it back in analog mode with MODER = 11, then touch the wire again and compare.
  3. The LED. LD2 is lit during Run and stays lit in the starter's low-power mode, so turn it off before sleeping. LD2 is driven through a transistor (UM3062 section 6.5), so measure how much this change saves and explain the result from where the LED's current comes from.

projects/bench12-sleep-current/solution steps through Run, Sleep and Stop 2 with all three changes. Fill in a table with the mode, the before and after readings and the change that made the difference.

You have measured the current your firmware costs and cut it with changes you can name, lesson 75's battery calculation with your own numbers in it.

Common mistakes and how to spot them

The debugger left attached

The Stop current stays high, because a debug session can keep the debug clocks on in Stop (the DBGMCU_CR DBG_STOP bit). Flash by drag-and-drop and then press the black reset button B2, so that no debug session is running.

Sleeping before the last UART byte is out

The last character of a log line is cut off, because Stop 2 stops the USART's clock while the character is still in the shift register. Wait for USART_ISR_TC (transmission complete) before sleeping, as both programs' _write() do before returning.

The meter on the wrong range

A 3 µA current shows as 0.00 on the 200 mA range, and a 3 mA Run current resets the chip on the 200 µA range, because each range suits only one end of the scale. Use mA for Run and µA for sleep.

JP4 not put back

The board looks dead while the ST-LINK still enumerates, because without the jumper or the meter the STM32 has no supply. Refit JP4 when you finish.

Summary

  • JP4 on the Nucleo-U5 isolates the microcontroller's supply, so removing it and putting an ammeter in its place measures the chip alone.
  • Sleep stops only the core's clock, whereas Stop 2 stops nearly every clock and keeps SRAM and registers, so it saves far more.
  • A digital input left floating draws current, and analog mode turns its input buffer off.
  • One change at a time with a measurement after each gives a table of before and after, which is the report.
ST's stm32u545xx.h, the PWR_CR1 bit definitions near the LPMS field: grep -n "PWR_CR1_" third_party/cmsis-device-u5/Include/stm32u545xx.h. The field is three bits wide; match the values to the mode names in the reference manual RM0456's PWR chapter.
