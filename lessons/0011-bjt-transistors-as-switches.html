BJT transistors as switches

A GPIO pin can switch a few milliamps. A transistor lets a small base current control a much larger collector current, which is how a pin switches a relay, a fan or a heater.

By the end of this lesson you can size a base resistor for an NPN switch on a 3.3 V GPIO, explain saturation, and say why a pin cannot drive a relay coil directly.

The idea

An NPN bipolar junction transistor (BJT) has three terminals: the base, the collector and the emitter. The base-emitter junction is a diode, so it conducts once the base is about 0.7 V above the emitter. In that state a small base current IB controls a much larger collector current IC. The ratio is the current gain, written β or hFE: IC = β × IB. The gain is not a constant. It depends on the current, the temperature and the part, and a datasheet gives a range. Design with the worst case, not the typical number. The All About Circuits textbook covers the transistor's operating regions in detail.

Used as a switch, the transistor has two states. Cut-off: the base current is zero, the collector current is zero, and the transistor is open. Saturation: the base current is large enough that the collector-emitter voltage falls to a small fraction of a volt, and the transistor behaves as a closed switch. You want saturation, not the in-between region, because in between the transistor wastes power as heat.

Worked example with real numbers

Goal: switch a 5 V relay coil of 100 Ω from a Pico GPIO at 3.3 V.

  1. Collector current. In saturation the collector-emitter voltage is small, say 0.2 V. IC = (5 V − 0.2 V) ÷ 100 Ω = 48 mA. The GPIO cannot supply that much, which is the reason for the transistor.
  2. Base current. Pick the base current so the forced gain IC ÷ IB is about 10 or less. The real β is then higher than the forced gain, so the transistor stays saturated when β varies. IB = 48 mA ÷ 10 = 4.8 mA is the minimum.
  3. Base resistor. The base-emitter junction takes about 0.7 V, so RB = (3.3 V − 0.7 V) ÷ IB. For 4.8 mA, that is 2.6 V ÷ 0.0048 A = 542 Ω. Choose the E12 value at or below 542 Ω, which is 470 Ω. Rounding down here gives more base current, which is the safe direction for a switch.
  4. Check. With 470 Ω, IB = 2.6 V ÷ 470 Ω = 5.5 mA, and the forced gain is 48 ÷ 5.5 = 8.7, which is below 10. The pin must source 5.5 mA. The RP2040's default drive is 4 mA, so set the pad to 8 mA. RP2040 datasheet, section 2.19.4

Why a pin cannot drive the coil directly

The coil needs 48 mA. The RP2040 pad's highest drive setting is 12 mA, and the total across the IOVDD bank is limited to 50 mA for all pins together. The pin could only supply a small part of what the coil needs, and it would hold its voltage poorly while doing it. The transistor is the amplifier: the pin provides the small base current, and the supply provides the coil current.

A transistor switching an inductive load (a relay coil or a motor) needs a flyback diode across the load, or the coil's back-EMF will destroy the transistor when it switches off. Lesson 14 covers this.

Hint

Check that the collector current is close to (5 V minus the collector-emitter drop) ÷ 100 Ω. If it is near 5 V ÷ 100 Ω = 50 mA, the transistor is saturated.

"A microcontroller must switch a 12 V fan that draws 200 mA. Design the output stage, and say what you would check on the pin." Answer out loud in 60 seconds, then compare.

Model answer

A GPIO cannot supply 200 mA, so switch the fan with a transistor. A BJT works with a base resistor sized for saturation, using about a tenth of the load current as base current, and the pin must supply that base current within its limit. For a fan this size, a MOSFET (lesson 12) is more usual, because it needs almost no gate current. If the load is inductive, add a flyback diode. Check the pin's drive strength and the total current limit of its supply bank.

PN2222A datasheet (Micro Commercial Components): read the absolute maximum ratings (VCEO 40 V, IC 600 mA, PD 625 mW) and the electrical characteristics table for DC current gain. Use the test conditions in the table, because the gain depends on them.
