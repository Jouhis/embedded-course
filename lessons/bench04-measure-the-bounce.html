Bench lab 4: Measure the bounce

Lesson 52 picked a debounce time. Here you measure how long a real button actually bounces, with the firmware and with a logic analyzer, and set the debounce from data.

This bench lab is optional. It needs a Pico 2, one or two pushbuttons (different kinds if you have them) and the logic analyzer. It doesn't count towards your progress. Time: about 45 minutes.
By the end of this lab you can capture a switch's bounce, measure its worst case over many presses, and choose a debounce lockout with a stated margin.

Why the simulator's number isn't yours

A mechanical switch closes by bringing two metal contacts together. They hit, spring apart and hit again several times before they settle, and each hit is an edge on the pin: bounce. Lesson 52 filtered it with a lockout or an integrator, sized by a rule of thumb.

Wokwi's pushbutton bounces too, but by a modelled amount: about 1 ms, and it can be turned off with the attribute bounce: "0" (Wokwi docs: wokwi-pushbutton). Real switches vary by type, by age and from press to press; some cheap tactile switches bounce for several milliseconds, and a worn one longer. A debounce tuned in the simulator can be far too short for the switch you actually fit.

How the firmware measures it

The program in projects/bench04-measure-the-bounce enables a GPIO interrupt on both edges of GP14. The handler does almost nothing, as lesson 57 recommends: it stores time_us_32() in an array and, on a falling edge outside the lockout window, counts a press. The main loop waits until no edge has come for 50 ms, then reports the burst: how many edges, and the time from the first to the last. One press makes one burst, and one release makes another. 

if ((events & GPIO_IRQ_EDGE_FALL) && (now - last_accept_us) >= DEBOUNCE_MS * 1000u) {
    last_accept_us = now;
    presses++;
}

The starter sets DEBOUNCE_MS to 1, about Wokwi's bounce. The program also prints a warning when one burst counted more than one press, so you'll see directly when the lockout is too short.

The interrupt has limits as a measuring tool. Edges closer together than the handler's run time can merge, so very fast bounce may be undercounted. The logic analyzer has no such limit, which is why you measure with both.

Stage 1: in Wokwi

  1. Build and run the starter. Click the button about ten times.
  2. Each burst reports a few edges over a span below about 1 ms, and the press count matches your clicks. With a 1 ms lockout, the simulator says the debounce works.

Stage 2: on the board

Wire the button from GP14 to GND, and the analyzer's D0 to GP14 and GND to GND. Flash the starter and open the serial port.

  1. Press and release the button 20 times, some slowly, some hard, some soft. Watch for warning: one burst counted N presses.
  2. Write down the worst so far value for presses and releases. Releases often bounce differently from presses.
  3. Capture a press in PulseView at 24 MHz. Zoom in on the first edge: count the edges and measure from the first to the last. Compare with the firmware's figure for the same press. If the analyzer shows more edges, the interrupt missed some.
  4. If you have a second kind of switch, repeat with it. A big arcade button and a small tactile switch rarely bounce alike.
  5. Choose DEBOUNCE_MS: the worst span you measured, doubled, rounded up to a whole millisecond, and at least 5 ms. Fill in the table below, set the value, rebuild, and press 20 more times: no warnings.

Why double it? Twenty presses are a small sample, and the switch will age. The margin costs little: a 20 ms lockout still allows 50 presses a second, faster than anyone presses a button. The cost appears only if the lockout grows long enough to eat genuine fast double presses, at around 100 ms.

Common mistakes

  1. Tuning on the simulator. Wokwi's bounce is a model setting, not your switch. Measure the switch you ship.
  2. Measuring only presses. The release bounces too, and with a lockout only on presses, release bounce can look like a new press when it rings below the threshold. Look at both.
  3. Doing the work in the interrupt. A handler that prints or debounces with delays misses edges and distorts the measurement. Timestamp and return.
  4. Too few samples. Bounce varies from press to press; one capture tells you almost nothing about the worst case.

Summary

  • Real switches bounce for longer, and more variably, than Wokwi's 1 ms model.
  • Timestamp edges in a minimal interrupt handler; report bursts from the main loop.
  • A logic analyzer sees edges an interrupt may merge, so cross-check with it.
  • Size the lockout from the measured worst case, with a margin, and recheck after the change.
Your debounce constant now has a measurement behind it, not a guess. You also have a small edge-timing tool you can reuse on any digital signal.
