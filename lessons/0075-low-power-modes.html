Low-power modes

A battery sensor spends most of its life asleep. Your job is to make sleep deep enough, and to wake the chip only when there is work, without losing a button press.

By the end of this lesson you can say what WFI, WFE, Sleep, Stop and Standby do on the STM32F1, name the wake-up sources you would use, and calculate a battery's life from its capacity and a duty-cycled average current.
Concept-only lesson. The Wokwi simulator does not model the PWR peripheral on the Blue Pill, and it does not implement the RTC or the low-power modes. So there is no Wokwi project here. The exercises are calculations. You can check the mode sequence on a real Blue Pill or an STM32 Nucleo board, with a meter or a power analyser.

The idea

A Cortex-M core sleeps when it executes WFI (wait for interrupt) or WFE (wait for event). It stops fetching instructions, and its clocks are gated, until a wake-up condition arrives. The difference is what wakes it. WFI wakes on an enabled interrupt. WFE wakes on an event, which is an interrupt or an event signal from a peripheral, and the event flag must be cleared as well. The wake-up latency is not zero. The core must restart its clocks and the voltage regulator may need to settle, so a deeper sleep has a longer wake-up time. Measure it on your board before you put a deadline on it.

Sleep, Stop and Standby

The STM32F1 has three levels, described in the Power control chapter of RM0008. Sleep stops the CPU clock and keeps the rest of the chip running, so peripherals and their interrupts still work. Stop stops the main clocks and keeps the SRAM and registers, and it can be woken by an EXTI line. Standby is the deepest level: the SRAM content is lost, and the chip restarts from reset on wake-up.

The core's SCB->SCR register selects the deep form. SLEEPDEEP makes WFI and WFE enter Stop or Standby, and SLEEPONEXIT makes the core sleep again after each exception handler returns. The STM32 PWR registers choose between Stop and Standby (PWR_CR bits PDDS and LPDS), and PWR_CSR bit WUF reports a wake-up. The register names are in stm32f103xb.h and core_cm3.h. RM0008, chapter "Power control (PWR)" and its low-power mode table

Standby loses the SRAM, so anything you need after the wake-up must be in backup registers or flash. It also restarts the chip from reset, so the wake-up path is the reset path. Design the state you keep before you choose Standby.

Wake-up sources

Each mode accepts only some wake-up sources. An EXTI line (lesson 70) works in Stop, because the EXTI logic keeps running on a low-speed clock. A timer or an ADC interrupt does not, because its clock stops in Stop. An RTC alarm is the usual way to wake from Standby on a timer. The RTC is not simulated in Wokwi, so you would check its wake-up path on hardware. The wake-up source table is in the PWR chapter of RM0008.

The power budget

The average current of a duty-cycled device is the time-weighted mean of its states. With an active current Ia for a fraction d of the time, and a sleep current Is for the rest: 

I_avg = d × I_a + (1 − d) × I_s
life (hours) = battery capacity (mAh) ÷ I_avg (mA)

The formula assumes an ideal battery. Real cells lose capacity at high current, self-discharge, and fall below the regulator's dropout. The sensor's current figures come from its datasheets, not from this lesson. The course does not quote current figures for the STM32F103: read the electrical characteristics table of the datasheet for the mode you use, at the voltage and temperature you run at. The duty cycle is the time spent awake divided by the period. Shortening the awake time usually helps more than a small change in the sleep current, so measure the awake time first.

Model answer

Start from the budget. Measure the awake time and its current, and use the average-current formula to find how much the awake time may cost. Keep the awake time short: sample, transmit and go back to sleep, with the main loop built around WFI, and SLEEPONEXIT for an interrupt-driven design. Choose the deepest mode that keeps the state you need: Stop keeps SRAM and wakes on EXTI, Standby loses SRAM and needs the RTC or an external pin to wake. Check the wake-up latency for each mode, and make sure no event is lost while the clocks restart. Then verify the design on hardware with a power analyser, because the datasheet current is a typical or maximum figure, not your board's.
