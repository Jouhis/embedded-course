Bench lab 13: Lab 4 on the Nucleo-U5

Lab 4 promised that a new board means one new file under hal.h and no change above it. Here you test the promise on a board the application has never seen: a real STM32U5, with real parts on a breadboard.

This bench lab is optional, and a stretch. It needs the NUCLEO-U545RE-Q from bench lab 9 and Lab 4's parts: an SSD1306 I2C OLED, two 4.7 kΩ resistors, two pushbuttons, a 10 kΩ NTC thermistor and a 10 kΩ resistor. It doesn't count towards your progress. It comes in three parts of under an hour each; stop after any of them.
By the end of this lab you can write a board layer for hal.h at register level on a new chip, bring up its I2C peripheral with timeouts, and run Lab 4's unchanged application on it.

What stays and what's new

The project projects/bench13-lab4-on-the-nucleo builds Lab 4's app/ folder in place: app.c, ui.c, display.c, ssd1306.c, format.c, font.c. Not one of them is copied or edited. Next to them are two new files:

  • hal_nucleo_u5.c, the board layer: hal_init(), the LED, the buttons, I2C, the millisecond clock and the log. It's register-level CMSIS, as in bench labs 9 to 12; ST's U5 HAL library isn't part of the course's build. The course's own hal.h is the abstraction (lesson 76).
  • sensor_ntc_u5.c, the sensor. Lab 4's sensor.c talks to the MYS-1, a custom chip that exists only in Wokwi. On the bench, a thermistor answers the same sensor.h interface, as it did on the Pico in bench lab 8.

Look at CMakeLists.txt: sensor.c is the only file of app/ left out of the build, and it's replaced behind its header. That's the second abstraction in the project doing its job.

Part 1 on the board: the frame and the GPIO

  1. Build and flash the starter (build/monitor.bin onto the ST-LINK drive). The VCP prints LAB4 init failed: the log and the millisecond clock already work, and the I2C functions return −1.
  2. In hal_init(), make PA5 an output and the two button pins inputs with pull-ups. The helpers pin_mode() and pin_pull() are written; the register names are bench lab 9's.
  3. Write hal_gpio_write() and hal_gpio_read(). Lab 4's contract: 1 means "on" and "pressed", whatever the wiring. LD2 is active high; the buttons pull their pins low.

To test Part 1 alone, put a temporary hal_gpio_write(HAL_PIN_LED, hal_gpio_read(HAL_PIN_BTN_A)); loop at the top of main(). Remove it before Part 2.

Part 2 on the board: I2C

The U5's I2C peripheral is a different design from the F1's. It runs a whole transfer in hardware: write the address, the byte count NBYTES, the direction RD_WRN, AUTOEND and START into CR2 in one go, then feed TXDR each time TXIS is set, or read RXDR each time RXNE is set. STOPF marks the end, NACKF a device that didn't answer.

  1. In hal_init(): clock I2C1 (RCC_APB1ENR1_I2C1EN), make PB6 and PB7 open-drain alternate-function pins with AF4, write TIMINGR, then set PE.
  2. TIMINGR for 100 kHz. The kernel clock is PCLK1, 4 MHz. With PRESC = 0 one step is 250 ns: SCLL = 19 gives a 5 µs low time and SCLH = 15 a 4 µs high time, and SDADEL = 2, SCLDEL = 4 set the data hold and setup times. Together with the rise time this is a period of about 10 µs.
  3. Write hal_i2c_write() and hal_i2c_read(). Every wait needs a timeout (I2C_TIMEOUT_MS), or a missing OLED hangs the monitor: Lab 4's hal.h says the call "never waits forever".

With the OLED connected, the VCP prints LAB4 OK and the display shows Lab 4's screen, with the temperature as an error (the sensor is Part 3). Now test the timeouts: pull the OLED's SDA wire while the monitor runs. display_flush() fails and the app skips the frame, so the display freezes, but the log lines keep coming and the buttons still work. Plug the wire back in and the display normally catches up; a transfer cut in the middle can leave it showing garbage until you press reset. Without timeouts the first failed transfer would hang the whole monitor.

Part 3 on the board: the NTC on ADC1

The U5's ADC1 is a 14-bit converter, 0 to 16 383, and it starts switched off more deeply than the F1's (lesson 58). sensor_ntc_u5.c has the conversion; the bring-up is yours:

  1. Clock the ADC (RCC_AHB2ENR1_ADC12EN) and the PWR block, and set PWR_SVMCR_ASV: the U5 keeps its analog supply domain isolated until the program says VDDA is present.
  2. Clear ADC_CR_DEEPPWD, set ADC_CR_ADVREGEN, and wait for ADC_ISR_LDORDY: the ADC's own voltage regulator is up.
  3. Calibrate: set ADC_CR_ADCAL and wait until it clears.
  4. Select channel 5: on the U5's ADC1 a channel must be preselected in PCSEL as well as listed in SQR1. Give it the longest sampling time in SMPR1: the divider's source resistance is 5 kΩ.
  5. Enable (ADEN), wait for ADRDY, and in sensor_poll() start 16 conversions with ADSTART, wait for EOC each time, and average.

The log now prints LAB4 T=... lines; hold the NTC between your fingers and watch the value climb. If sensor_init() fails, print which wait timed out: each step's flag tells you how far the bring-up got.

Lab 4's promise held on real hardware you've never targeted before. That's what a hardware abstraction layer is for, and now you have the evidence.

Common mistakes
  1. Editing a file in app/ to make it work. Then the port is no longer a port. If the application needs something new from the board, the change belongs in hal.h and in every board's file.
  2. Push-pull I2C pins. With OTYPER left at push-pull, the pin drives the line high against a device pulling it low. Set both pins open-drain.
  3. No pull-ups. Open-drain lines with no pull-up never go high; every transfer times out. Check with the multimeter: SDA and SCL idle at 3.3 V.
  4. Forgetting PCSEL. The conversion runs and returns a value that has nothing to do with the voltage on the pin.

Summary

  • The Lab 4 application ran on a third board with one new board file and one replaced sensor file behind an unchanged header.
  • The U5's I2C runs a whole transfer from one write to CR2; the driver feeds or drains bytes and checks NACKF, STOPF and a timeout.
  • TIMINGR encodes SCL's low and high times and the data setup and hold times in prescaled kernel-clock steps.
  • ADC1 on the U5 needs its analog domain declared valid, its regulator started, a calibration, and the channel preselected before it converts.
The I2C_CR2_ definitions in third_party/cmsis-device-u5/Include/stm32u545xx.h (grep -n "I2C_CR2_" ...): about 30 lines that list every field you wrote in one go in Part 2. Compare with the F1's I2C_CR1_START and I2C_SR1 sequence in the Blue Pill HAL.
