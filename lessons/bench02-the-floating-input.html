Bench lab 2: The floating input

An input with nothing driving it reads a steady value in Wokwi. On a real Pico 2 it reads noise that changes when your hand comes near. Then meet a chip erratum that breaks the obvious fix on some boards.

This bench lab is optional. It needs a Pico 2, a pushbutton, jumper wires, and for part 3 a 4.7 kΩ or 8.2 kΩ resistor. It doesn't count towards your progress. Time: about 45 minutes.
By the end of this lab you can show a floating input on a real board, fix it with the right pull resistor, and tell from CHIP_ID whether your RP2350 has the pull-down erratum.

What lesson 16 said, and what the simulator shows

Lesson 16 explained that a CMOS input draws almost no current, so an input connected to nothing has no defined voltage: it floats. A button to GND only defines the level while it is pressed. When it's released, the pin is connected to nothing, and something must pull it to the idle level: a pull-up.

The simulator can't show you why. A floating wire picks up charge from nearby fields, from your hand, from the next wire, and the input's tiny capacitance holds it. Wokwi has no stray fields and no hands, so a floating input simply reads a fixed value. The program logic looks correct, and the bug ships.

The project, projects/bench02-floating-input, samples GP16 a thousand times a second and prints, once a second, how many samples were high and how many times the value changed. A pin with a defined level gives changes=0. A floating one doesn't.

Stage 1: in Wokwi

  1. Open the starter. INPUT_PULL is PULL_NONE: no pull resistor at all.
  2. Build and run it. The log shows a steady result every second, the same one every time. Press the button in the diagram and the count changes; release it and it goes back.
  3. By the simulator's evidence, the code works without a pull resistor. Keep that thought.

Stage 2: on the board, floating

Wire a pushbutton between GP16 and a GND pin, with a 15 to 20 cm jumper to GP16 so there's some wire to act as an antenna. Flash the starter's UF2 and open the serial port.

  1. Don't touch anything for a few seconds. Note highs and changes. They may be steady, or they may wander.
  2. Bring a finger close to the GP16 wire without touching it, then touch its insulation, then touch the bare metal of the button's leg. Watch changes jump.
  3. Press and hold the button: highs=0/1000 changes=0. The button defines the level, so the pin is solid. Release it and the noise returns.

What you just saw is the input's stray capacitance, a few picofarads, being charged and discharged by your body's field. Mains wiring in the walls adds a 50 Hz component (bench lab 17 measures that on purpose). The behaviour also depends on the room, the weather and the board, which makes floating-input bugs notorious: they fail on one desk and pass on another.

Stage 3: the fix, and the trap

Set INPUT_PULL to PULL_UP, rebuild and flash. Now an idle pin reads highs=1000/1000 changes=0 and the log prints BENCH2 stable high, no matter where your hand is. The internal pull-up is 32 to 86 kΩ on the RP2350 (RP2350 datasheet, section 14.9.4): weak enough that a press barely draws current (about 0.1 mA), strong enough to swamp the stray charge.

Now the trap. Some designs wire the button the other way, from the pin to 3V3, and use the pull-down so that pressed reads 1. Try it: move the button's GND leg to a 3V3 pin, set INPUT_PULL to PULL_DOWN, rebuild and flash. Look at the first log line, which prints the CHIP_ID revision field you met in bench lab 0.

  • Revision field 3 (steppings A3 and A4): idle reads 0, pressed reads 1. The pull-down works.
  • Revision field 2 (stepping A2, the first boards sold): press the button once and release it. The pin stays high.

That second case is erratum RP2350-E9. On A2 chips, when a pin is an input and its voltage sits between VIL and VIH, the pad leaks current, typically around 120 µA, which holds the pad near 2.2 V. The internal pull-down is far too weak to overcome it, so once the pin has been high, it doesn't come back down. The datasheet's fix is an external pull-down of 8.2 kΩ or less, which pulls the pin below the region where the leak occurs; the pull-up is unaffected, because it pulls the pin out of that region from the other side. Stepping A3 removes the leak (RP2350 datasheet, appendix D.5.1 and appendix C).

On an A2 board, add a 4.7 kΩ resistor from GP16 to GND and repeat: the pin now returns to 0 when you release the button. In Wokwi none of this exists: the simulator models a clean pull-down, which is why an errata sheet is part of the documentation you read before you choose pins and pulls.

Before you wire anything to 5 V: the RP2350's digital GPIOs tolerate up to 5.5 V on an input, but only while IOVDD is powered (RP2350 datasheet, section 14.9.4). With the board unplugged, a 5 V signal on a pin is out of spec. The ADC pins, GPIO26 to 29, aren't 5 V tolerant at all: they have a diode to IOVDD (Pico 2 datasheet, section 5.2). Keep this lab at 3.3 V.

Common mistakes

  1. Trusting a quiet desk. A floating pin can read steady for minutes and then flip when someone walks past. changes=0 without a pull resistor proves nothing.
  2. Pull-down on an A2 board. Symptom: the input sticks high after the first press. Fix: use a pull-up with active-low logic, or an external pull-down of 8.2 kΩ or less.
  3. Pull in the wrong direction. A pull-up with the button to 3V3 never reads low; a pull-down with the button to GND never reads high. The pull goes to the opposite rail from the button.
  4. Assuming every RP2350 is the same. Erratum behaviour depends on the stepping; log CHIP_ID at boot so bug reports say which silicon they came from.

Summary

  • An input with nothing driving it floats; on a real board it reads noise that depends on hands, wires and the room.
  • Wokwi reads a floating input as a fixed value, so this bug only shows on the bench.
  • A pull resistor to the idle rail defines the level; the RP2350's internal pull-up is 32 to 86 kΩ.
  • On RP2350 A2 (revision field 2), erratum E9 defeats the internal pull-down; use a pull-up, or an external pull-down of 8.2 kΩ or less. A3 and later are fixed.
  • Digital GPIOs are 5 V tolerant only while IOVDD is powered; the ADC pins never are.
