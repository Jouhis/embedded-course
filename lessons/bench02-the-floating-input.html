Bench lab 2: The floating input

Undriven inputs, pull resistors and a silicon erratum

By the end of this lab you can show a floating input on a real board, fix it with the right pull resistor, and tell from CHIP_ID whether your RP2350 has the pull-down erratum.

What lesson 16 said, and what the simulator shows

An input with nothing driving it reads a steady value in Wokwi, whereas on a real Pico 2 it reads noise that changes when your hand comes near, and this lab shows that difference before meeting a chip erratum that breaks the obvious fix on some boards. It is optional, needing a Pico 2, a pushbutton, jumper wires and, for part 3, a 4.7 kΩ or 8.2 kΩ resistor, it does not count towards your progress, and it takes about 45 minutes.

Lesson 16 explained that a CMOS input draws almost no current, so an input connected to nothing has no defined voltage but floats, and a button to GND defines the level only while it is pressed, so when it is released, the pin is connected to nothing and something must pull it to the idle level, a pull-up.

The simulator cannot show why, because a floating wire picks up charge from nearby fields, from your hand and from the next wire, which the input's tiny capacitance holds, whereas Wokwi has no stray fields and no hands, so a floating input simply reads a fixed value, the program logic looks correct, and the bug ships.

The project, projects/bench02-floating-input, samples GP16 a thousand times a second and prints once a second how many samples were high and how many times the value changed, so a pin with a defined level gives changes=0, whereas a floating one does not.

Stage 1: in Wokwi

  1. Open the starter, in which INPUT_PULL is PULL_NONE, no pull resistor at all.
  2. Build and run it, the log showing the same steady result every second, and pressing the button in the diagram changing the count, which goes back when you release it.
  3. By the simulator's evidence the code works without a pull resistor, a thought to keep for the next stage.

Stage 2: on the board, floating

Wire a pushbutton between GP16 and a GND pin, with a 15 to 20 cm jumper to GP16, so that there is some wire to act as an antenna, then flash the starter's UF2 and open the serial port.

  1. Touch nothing for a few seconds and note highs and changes, which may be steady or may wander.
  2. Bring a finger close to the GP16 wire without touching it, then touch its insulation and then the bare metal of the button's leg, watching changes jump.
  3. Press and hold the button, which gives highs=0/1000 changes=0, because the button defines the level and so the pin is solid, and release it to see the noise return.

What you just saw is the input's stray capacitance, a few picofarads, being charged and discharged by your body's field, mains wiring in the walls adding a 50 Hz component (bench lab 17 measures that on purpose), and since the behaviour also depends on the room, the weather and the board, floating-input bugs are notorious, failing on one desk and passing on another.

Stage 3: the fix, and the trap

Setting INPUT_PULL to PULL_UP, rebuilding and flashing makes an idle pin read highs=1000/1000 changes=0 and the log print BENCH2 stable high, wherever your hand is, because the RP2350's internal pull-up of 32 to 86 kΩ (RP2350 datasheet, section 14.9.4) is weak enough that a press barely draws current, about 0.1 mA, yet strong enough to swamp the stray charge.

The trap comes with designs that wire the button the other way, from the pin to 3V3, and use the pull-down, so that pressed reads 1. Try it by moving the button's GND leg to a 3V3 pin, setting INPUT_PULL to PULL_DOWN, rebuilding and flashing, and then look at the first log line, which prints the CHIP_ID revision field you met in bench lab 0.

  • Revision field 3 (steppings A3 and A4): idle reads 0, pressed reads 1. The pull-down works.
  • Revision field 2 (stepping A2, the first boards sold): press the button once and release it. The pin stays high.

That second case is erratum RP2350-E9, in which on A2 chips a pin that is an input with its voltage between VIL and VIH leaks current at the pad, typically around 120 µA, holding the pad near 2.2 V, which the internal pull-down is far too weak to overcome, so once the pin has been high, it does not come back down. The datasheet's fix is an external pull-down of 8.2 kΩ or less, pulling the pin below the region where the leak occurs, while the pull-up is unaffected, because it pulls the pin out of that region from the other side, and stepping A3 removes the leak (RP2350 datasheet, appendix D.5.1 and appendix C).

On an A2 board, adding a 4.7 kΩ resistor from GP16 to GND and repeating makes the pin return to 0 when you release the button, whereas in Wokwi none of this exists, because the simulator models a clean pull-down, which is why an errata sheet is part of the documentation you read before choosing pins and pulls.

Before you wire anything to 5 V: the RP2350's digital GPIOs tolerate up to 5.5 V on an input, but only while IOVDD is powered (RP2350 datasheet, section 14.9.4). With the board unplugged, a 5 V signal on a pin is out of spec. The ADC pins, GPIO26 to 29, aren't 5 V tolerant at all: they have a diode to IOVDD (Pico 2 datasheet, section 5.2). Keep this lab at 3.3 V.
You have seen a bug the simulator cannot show, fixed it and found an erratum by reading a chip's revision register, the whole bench-lab idea in one exercise.

Common mistakes and how to spot them

A quiet desk taken as proof

A floating pin reads steady for minutes and then flips when someone walks past, because the stray charge depends on the surroundings, so changes=0 without a pull resistor proves nothing. Always define the idle level with a pull resistor.

A pull-down on an A2 board

The input sticks high after the first press, because erratum E9 defeats the internal pull-down. Use a pull-up with active-low logic, or an external pull-down of 8.2 kΩ or less.

A pull in the wrong direction

A pull-up with the button to 3V3 never reads low, and a pull-down with the button to GND never reads high, because the pull and the button go to the same rail. Put the pull on the opposite rail from the button.

Every RP2350 assumed to be the same

A bug report cannot be reproduced on another board, because erratum behaviour depends on the stepping. Log CHIP_ID at boot, so that bug reports say which silicon they came from.

Summary

  • An input with nothing driving it floats, and on a real board it reads noise that depends on hands, wires and the room.
  • Wokwi reads a floating input as a fixed value, so this bug shows only on the bench.
  • A pull resistor to the idle rail defines the level, the RP2350's internal pull-up being 32 to 86 kΩ.
  • On RP2350 A2 (revision field 2) erratum E9 defeats the internal pull-down, so use a pull-up or an external pull-down of 8.2 kΩ or less, A3 and later being fixed.
  • Digital GPIOs are 5 V tolerant only while IOVDD is powered, and the ADC pins never are.
