Explain out loud

Say each answer in 60 to 90 seconds, as if to a junior engineer, then open the model answer.

1. "Your timer ISR and your UART ISR both touch one ring buffer index. What can go wrong, and how do you fix it?"

Model answer

The index is a read-modify-write, so an interrupt between the load and the store can lose an update. If the two ISRs can preempt each other, one of them can change the index in the middle of the other's update. The fix is to give each index one writer, or to protect the update with a short critical section (PRIMASK, as in lesson 72's log), and to keep the protected code to a few instructions. Lesson 70 and lesson 72 cover this.

2. "A 1 kHz control loop on a Blue Pill occasionally runs 30 ms late. Walk through what you check, in order."

Model answer

Check the priorities first: a long, low-priority handler can delay a timer that is numerically higher, so fix the grouping and the priority numbers (lesson 72). Then check the time base: a HAL_Delay or a blocking call at a high priority can hang, and SysTick must not be starved (lesson 73). Then check the work in the handler: move anything slow out of the ISR into the main loop. Measure with a timestamp at each entry and exit, because only a measured drop in latency shows that the fix worked. Lesson 71's timer can give you that timestamp with a 1 MHz count.

3. "A battery sensor must last a year. What do you calculate first, and what does the datasheet not tell you?"

Model answer

Start with the duty cycle and the awake time. The average current is the time-weighted mean of the active and sleep currents, and the battery life is the capacity divided by that average (lesson 75). The datasheet gives typical or maximum current for a stated condition, but not for your board, your regulator, your sensor or your radio. So measure the real awake time and the real currents with a power analyser, and add margin for cell capacity loss at low temperature and high current. Lesson 74's DMA can cut the awake time when the data arrives in bursts.
