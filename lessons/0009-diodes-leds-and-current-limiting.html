Diodes, LEDs, and current-limiting resistors

An LED does not limit its own current, so the resistor in front of it has to. Choosing that resistor is the first real decision you make about a GPIO pin.

By the end of this lesson you can choose the resistor for an LED on a 3.3 V GPIO, using R = (Vsupply − Vf) ÷ I and an E12 value, and check the result against the RP2040 pin's drive limit.

The idea

A diode passes current in one direction. Below a threshold voltage it passes almost nothing. Above it, the current rises very steeply for a small voltage increase. The threshold is the forward voltage, Vf. A silicon diode's Vf is about 0.6 to 0.7 V, and the All About Circuits textbook covers semiconductors and this I–V curve. The steepness is the problem. Because the current rises so fast, a small change in voltage gives a large change in current. Connect a diode straight to a supply and the current is set by the supply's internal resistance, which can be far too high.

An LED is a diode that emits light. Its forward voltage depends on its colour, because the bandgap of the semiconductor sets the photon energy. A red LED is commonly about 1.8 to 2.2 V, according to a typical product listing. SparkFun basic red 5 mm LED. Always check the forward voltage in your own LED's datasheet.

Because the current is what sets the brightness and the life of an LED, you design around current. The series resistor makes the supply and the LED share the voltage. The LED takes Vf, and the resistor takes the rest:

R = (Vsupply − Vf) ÷ I

Worked example with real numbers

A Pico GPIO drives a red LED at 3.3 V. Assume Vf = 2.0 V, and target 4 mA, which is the default drive strength of the pin (see below). Then:

  1. Voltage across the resistor: 3.3 V − 2.0 V = 1.3 V.
  2. Exact resistance: 1.3 V ÷ 0.004 A = 325 Ω.
  3. E12 value: the standard values near 325 Ω are 270 Ω and 330 Ω. Round up to 330 Ω, so the current stays at or below the target.
  4. Actual current: 1.3 V ÷ 330 Ω = 0.0039 A, which is 3.9 mA.

E12 values. Resistors come in preferred-number steps, not in every value. The E12 series has twelve values per decade: 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 1.8, 2.2, 2.7, 3.3, 3.9, 4.7, 5.6, 6.8 and 8.2, each multiplied by a power of ten. The series is standardised in IEC 60063, and the DIN publication of EN 60063:2015 is the standard. Rounding up keeps the current below the target, which is the safe direction for an LED.

The GPIO limit

The pin can supply only so much current. In the RP2040 datasheet, each GPIO pad has a drive strength you choose from 2, 4, 8 or 12 mA, and the default is 4 mA. RP2040 datasheet, section 2.19.4 (Pads), and section 5.5.2.1 (default 4 mA). The datasheet also limits the total current across all the IOVDD pins to 50 mA (section 5.5.3.4, Table 625). The drive strength is set in the pad control register, described in section 2.19.6.3 of the same datasheet. Lesson 51 covers GPIO output from C.

Do not connect an LED straight to a GPIO with no resistor. The pin holds its output near 3.3 V, and once the LED is past its forward voltage, a small rise in voltage gives a large rise in current. Nothing but the pin's own resistance and the LED then limits the current, and that can exceed both the pin's drive limit and the LED's rating. The resistor is not optional.

You chose a series resistor from the supply, the forward voltage and a target current. You rounded to a real E12 value in the safe direction, and checked the pin's drive limit. That is the calculation behind almost every LED on a board.

"How would you choose the series resistor for an LED on a 3.3 V GPIO, and what would you check on the pin?" Answer out loud in 60 seconds, then compare.

Model answer

Subtract the LED's forward voltage from the supply, and divide by the target current, which is the current the LED should have. Round up to the next E12 value so the current stays at or below the target. Then check the pin: the drive strength must cover the current, and the default is 4 mA on the RP2040. The total across the IOVDD pins also has a limit of 50 mA.

RP2040 datasheet: section 2.19.4 (Pads) for the drive strengths 2, 4, 8 and 12 mA, and section 5.5.2.1 for the default of 4 mA. Read the pad description before you trust any drive-strength number.
