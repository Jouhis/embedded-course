Checkpoint 4: Peripherals on the Pico

Lessons 51 to 65 each add one peripheral or one technique. This checkpoint mixes them, so you have to choose the right tool for each question, not just recall the last lesson you read.

By the end of this checkpoint you can choose the right GPIO, timing, interrupt, communication or DMA technique for a given job, calculate the numbers each one needs, and explain in your own words why the other choices fail.

The RP2040 register facts in this checkpoint come from the RP2040 datasheet, and the Pico SDK functions from the Pico SDK documentation. Answer the questions without notes first. Each explanation names the lesson to revisit if you miss one. Then answer the explain-out-loud prompts at the end, as if to a colleague. The part of the course that each question covers is shown in the explanation, so you can tell which lesson to reopen.

Ask your teacher (the AI agent in this workspace) about anything you missed. Try: "Quiz me on Part 4 with five questions I have not seen, and tell me which lesson to revisit for each wrong answer." Revisit lessons 51 to 65 as needed. The most common misses are likely to be the interrupt and volatile rules (lessons 46 and 57), and the non-blocking timing (lesson 55). For the bus questions, see Lesson 60: The I2C protocol, Lesson 61: An I2C OLED display and Lesson 62: SPI. Also see Lesson 63 and Lab 3.
