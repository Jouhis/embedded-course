Lab 1: Design and simulate an LED and MOSFET load driver

A 3.3 V pin switches a 12 V, 24 W heater. You choose every component, justify each value from a datasheet, and check the result in CircuitJS.

By the end of this lab you can design a logic-level low-side MOSFET driver for a 12 V load, justify the gate resistor, pull-down and MOSFET parameters from datasheet numbers, and verify the load current and MOSFET dissipation in a simulator.

The brief

A microcontroller pin on 3.3 V must switch a 12 V heater that draws about 2 A, which is the kind of load a 3D printer's heated bed or a fan driver switches. The MOSFET sits on the low side, between the heater and ground, and the heater's other end is on the 12 V rail. The pin drives the gate, through a gate resistor, and a pull-down holds the gate low when the pin is not driving it. The heater is modelled as a 6 Ω resistor, which is an exact stand-in at a steady state. Use the low side (lesson 12): a 3.3 V pin cannot turn on a high-side N-channel device, because its gate would need to be above 12 V.

You will need three numbers from datasheets. The RP2040 datasheet gives the GPIO's default drive current of 4 mA (Table 614). The IRLZ44N datasheet gives the gate threshold range of 1.0 to 2.0 V, the on-resistance at 4 V and 5 V, and the thermal resistance of 1.4 °C/W from junction to case (Electrical Characteristics and Thermal Resistance). The IRLZ44N is a worked example here. Any logic-level MOSFET is acceptable if you justify it with its own datasheet.

Requirements

  1. The gate is driven from a 3.3 V GPIO through a gate resistor of your choice, and a gate pull-down resistor is connected from the gate to ground.
  2. The load is a 6 Ω resistor in series with the MOSFET drain, supplied from a 12 V rail.
  3. The MOSFET is a logic-level N-channel device. You set its threshold voltage to the worst-case datasheet maximum, and choose its β so that its on-resistance at 3.3 V is at most 0.1 Ω.
  4. You measure the load current, the MOSFET drain-source voltage and the MOSFET's dissipation, and they meet the acceptance criteria below.
  5. You write one sentence for each component value, saying which datasheet number or calculation justifies it.

Staged milestones

  1. Milestone 1, the load. Start from the starter netlist below. Set the drain supply to 12 V and the gate supply to 0 V. Confirm the drain voltage reads 12 V at the drain node.
  2. Milestone 2, the gate. Add the gate resistor and the pull-down. Justify both values in writing before you place them.
  3. Milestone 3, the MOSFET. Set the threshold to 2.0 V and β to a value that meets requirement 3. Show your calculation.
  4. Milestone 4, the measurements. Set the gate to 3.3 V and measure Iload, VDS and the dissipation. Check 1 to 5.
  5. Stretch. Replace the 6 Ω heater with a 0.1 H inductor in series with a 6 Ω resistor, add a flyback diode across it, and show the drain spike with and without it (lesson 14).

Reference values. The gate is at 3.3 V with no DC gate current, so Vov = 3.3 − 2.0 = 1.3 V. The MOSFET is in the triode region, so ID = β × (Vov × VDS − VDS² / 2) and 12 V = 6 Ω × ID + VDS. Solving those two equations gives VDS ≈ 0.16 V and ID ≈ 1.97 A. The dissipation is about 0.32 W, and the on-resistance at this bias is about 0.08 Ω, which meets requirement 3.

Hint: choosing β

The CircuitJS MOSFET model defines β = 1 ÷ (RDS(on) × Vov). Set RDS(on) to 0.1 Ω and Vov to 1.3 V, the worst case, and β = 1 ÷ (0.1 × 1.3) = 7.7. Any β at or above 7.7 meets requirement 3, and β = 10 gives margin.

Hint: the gate resistor

At a 3.3 V step, the peak current into the gate is 3.3 V ÷ Rgate. For 4 mA, Rgate must be at least 825 Ω, so 1 kΩ gives 3.3 mA. The gate draws no current in steady state, so the resistor does not change the DC gate voltage.

Hint: the pull-down

A 100 kΩ pull-down draws 3.3 V ÷ 100 kΩ = 33 µA from a pin that is high, which is small against the 4 mA drive. It holds the gate low when the pin is high impedance, which is the case during reset or when a driver is disconnected.

Hint: why the gate needs no current

The gate is insulated. Once it is charged, it draws no DC current, so the gate voltage is the pin voltage. The IRLZ44N datasheet gives Qg = 48 nC, so a 4 mA pin needs 48 nC ÷ 4 mA = 12 µs to switch the gate. That is fine for a heater, but too slow for fast PWM.
