Bench lab 8: Lab 3 on the bench

Run your Lab 3 environment monitor on a real Pico 2 with a real OLED, real buttons and a real temperature sensor. The simulated MYS-1 doesn't exist as a part, so a thermistor takes its place behind the same interface, and nothing above that interface changes.

This bench lab is optional. It needs a Pico 2, the SSD1306 OLED, two pushbuttons, a 10 kΩ NTC thermistor, a 10 kΩ resistor and two 4.7 kΩ resistors. It doesn't count towards your progress. It's split into three parts of under an hour each; stop after any part.
By the end of this lab you can replace a driver behind an existing interface without touching the code above it, and bring a multi-part project up on hardware one subsystem at a time.

The plan: swap one file

Lab 3 read the MYS-1, the course's own I2C sensor from lesson 63. It's a custom Wokwi chip: it exists only in the simulator, and you can't buy one. But Lab 3's main.c never talks to the MYS-1. It calls two functions from sensor.h: 

bool sensor_init(void);
sensor_status_t sensor_poll(float *celsius);   /* SENSOR_IDLE, SENSOR_NEW_SAMPLE or SENSOR_ERROR */

Any sensor that can fill in those two functions can replace it. The bench version uses a 10 kΩ NTC thermistor in a divider on ADC0 (GP26), wired the way Wokwi's NTC part is: 10 kΩ from 3V3 to GP26, the NTC from GP26 to GND. Lesson 58 converted that divider's reading to a temperature with the Beta equation. The new file is sensor_ntc.c; main.c, ui.c, display.c, ssd1306.c and font.c are Lab 3's, copied unchanged into projects/bench08-lab3-on-the-bench.

The contract has details worth keeping. sensor_poll() must return at once, never wait. It returns SENSOR_NEW_SAMPLE once per new reading, so the NTC version reports a new sample every 500 ms and SENSOR_IDLE in between. And a broken sensor must show as SENSOR_ERROR, not as a plausible wrong temperature, so the UI's error path still works.

Part 1: the board without the sensor (about 40 minutes)

  1. Open the starter in Wokwi, build and run. Its sensor_ntc.c returns SENSOR_ERROR. The OLED, the menu and both buttons work; the screen reports a sensor error. That's the expected state: everything except the sensor.
  2. On the bench, wire the OLED as in bench lab 6 (GP4 SDA, GP5 SCL, 4.7 kΩ pull-ups) and the buttons from GP14 and GP15 to GND. Flash the starter and open the serial port.
  3. Check each subsystem against what Wokwi did: the log prints LAB3 OK and T=none; the screen shows the error; button A changes screens, button B changes the setpoint.
  4. Press each button 20 times and watch for double steps. Lab 3's debounce is a 30 ms lockout; check it against the bounce you measured in bench lab 4.

If the screen stays black, that's bench lab 6's fault: Lab 3's display_init() can't report a missing panel. Scan the bus with bench lab 6's program before you change anything here.

Part 2: the NTC driver (about 50 minutes)

  1. Fill in sensor_init(): keep the I2C set-up (the OLED needs it), and add adc_init() and adc_gpio_init(26).
  2. Fill in sensor_poll(): if 500 ms haven't passed since the last sample, return SENSOR_IDLE. Otherwise read ADC0 16 times and average (bench lab 5), and convert with lesson 58's Beta equation.
  3. Treat a raw average within 16 codes of 0 or 4095 as SENSOR_ERROR: near 0 the NTC is shorted, near full scale it's missing.
  4. Build and run in Wokwi. The NTC part's default is 24 °C; the screen should show about that. Move its slider and the reading follows.
  5. Wire the real divider: 10 kΩ from 3V3 (pin 36) to GP26, the NTC from GP26 to AGND (pin 33). Flash and compare the reading with a room thermometer. Hold the NTC between two fingers: it should climb towards 30 °C within half a minute.
  6. Unplug the NTC: the screen must show the sensor error within a second, not a temperature.
float inv_t = logf(1.0f / ((float)FULL_SCALE / (float)raw - 1.0f)) / BETA + 1.0f / T0_KELVIN;
*celsius = 1.0f / inv_t - 273.15f;

Part 3: what the bench changed (about 30 minutes)

With everything working, compare your bench readings with the simulator's, and explain each difference. Typical findings:

  • An offset of a degree or two. Your thermistor's Beta may not be 3950 (3380 is also common), and the 10 kΩ resistor and the NTC each have a tolerance. Find the Beta on your part's listing or datasheet and change BETA. A two-point check, room air and a glass of ice water stirred for a minute (0 °C), shows how far off you are.
  • A last digit that flickers. ADC noise from bench lab 5. Without the 16-fold averaging it's worse; the alarm can chatter when the temperature sits at the setpoint. Lab 3's alarm has no hysteresis; consider adding 0.5 °C.
  • The ADC's offset. The Pico 2's ADC carries an offset of about 30 mV, from its own current through the 200 Ω supply filter resistor (Pico 2 datasheet, section 5.3); bench lab 5 measured it. At room temperature that is a fraction of a degree: small, but it explains part of the offset.
Your Lab 3 runs on real parts, and you changed exactly one file to get there. That's what the interface was for.

Common mistakes
  1. Editing main.c or ui.c to make the NTC fit. The point of the interface is that nothing above it changes. If you need to, the interface was wrong, and that's worth noting rather than hiding.
  2. Blocking in sensor_poll(). A sleep_ms(500) instead of the deadline check freezes the buttons and the screen for half a second each time.
  3. Reporting a temperature for an open sensor. A missing NTC reads near 4095, which the equation turns into a very cold reading. Range-check first.
  4. NTC to GND, not AGND. It works, but shares the digital ground's noise; AGND is the ADC's reference ground on the Pico 2.

Summary

  • A small interface (sensor_init, sensor_poll) let a different sensor replace a simulator-only one, with the rest of Lab 3 unchanged.
  • Bring a board up one subsystem at a time, checking each against what the simulator showed.
  • Keep the interface's contract: non-blocking, one sample reported once, faults as errors not values.
  • Real temperature readings carry Beta, tolerance, noise and offset errors; a two-point check measures them.
Your thermistor's own datasheet or listing: find its resistance at 25 °C and its Beta value (often written B25/50 or B25/85). Compare it with the 3950 the code assumes.
Ask your teacher (the AI agent in this workspace) about anything unclear. Try: "My NTC's datasheet gives B25/85 = 3435. How do I calibrate the code with my two measurements instead of trusting the datasheet value?" Related: Lab 3: Environment monitor, Lesson 58: ADC.
